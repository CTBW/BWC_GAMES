Updated 24 October 2026 from the user’s marked selection. The Giant Troll revision follows the supplied woodland troll species more closely, with living-stone surfaces, no ears and a shaggy dark beard. His smaller upright hulking silhouette and approved shoulder ruins remain central. The original unit sheets control painted style and the earlier terrain cottage controls ruin materials. The Pale Colossus replaces the buried Crown design. The user approved colossus take 3 and requested glowing aqua eyes. The colossus palette edit uses its pale first take; the focused head correction uses the darker second take. Take 4 edits only the eyes of the approved third take to glowing aqua. ## The Sanguine Delver

*Blood-crawler and fungal land-corruptor*

**Every wound you open becomes a wound in the land.**

**Tactical question:** Where can you afford to wound it—and which ground must survive long enough to win?

**Scale:** About 24 metres long; low, many-legged body; proposed 5 × 3 footprint; 26 × 24 arena.

**Appearance:** A long, low rust-red crawler with a broad armoured head, paired hooked mouthparts and many short splayed legs. Overlapping dark-red shell plates rise into a few heavy fungal shelves; blood-filled translucent sacs hang beneath the flanks. Its pale underside contrasts with the red-black shell. It remains recognisably a scuttling animal beneath the fungus.

**Place in the world:** Original boss inspired by the user’s small Drova creature reference, which shows a reddish, low, elongated crawler. Proposed Meridius ecology: Host tunnelling breaks into an old subterranean fungal colony; a centuries-old carrier reaches the surface, and its blood feeds a fungus that consumes the soil’s binding roots. The land slumps after the fungus hollows it. No claim about Drova’s actual lore or enemy mechanics is made.

**Arena:** A broad abandoned terraced quarry at the edge of exhausted farmland. Clay banks, shallow extraction channels and old stone haulways show progressive erosion clearly. An unbroken foundation-stone outer circuit is immune to the blood fungus; the valuable inner earth slowly becomes contested.

**Movement:** The crawler follows a forecast low S-shaped path toward a chosen blood-scent bait. Its whole swept body is marked. After two surface sequences it burrows shallowly, showing a moving earth ridge and an announced emergence footprint for a full turn. It cannot pass through stable foundation stone.

**Who takes which hit:** A sturdy frontal squad catches the maw while a separate pair holds pressure plates beside a fungal vent, dividing a rupture shock. A mobile squad carries the bait and preserves access to cauterising braziers. Shooters decide whether to cross a visible wound threshold now or wait until the crawler moves over expendable soil.

### Phases

1. The Carrier: one stain front teaches the slow erosion sequence before any overlapping eruption.
2. The Orchard of Blood, below roughly 65%: wound thresholds and vent assignments overlap. At most two active uncauterised erosion fronts advance at once; additional fronts wait visibly.
3. The Hollowing, below roughly 30%: the shell opens and the crawler burrows more often. Underside attacks cancel queued ruptures, so aggression can reclaim control of the terrain.

### Opening sequence

- Beat 1: Bait the crawler onto an outer earth terrace while an armoured squad prepares the stopping cell.
- Beat 2: Cross the first wound threshold there. The preview shows blood flowing away from the central haulway.
- Beat 3: The stain becomes Bloom. Two fresh squads hold the vent stations while a reserve reaches a brazier.
- Beat 4: Cauterise the most dangerous front; let the expendable terrace undercut. The next emergence exposes the underside and allows a queued rupture to be cancelled.

**Typical failure:** The company unloads every attack while the crawler straddles the central route, then discovers that its best damage turn has seeded the ground needed for the next two rotations.

**Recovery:** The stone outer circuit is permanently safe from erosion. Cauterising costs an act and access, not a rare class. Two-front scheduling bounds the puzzle; destroyed inner ground stays destroyed for the encounter, making earlier decisions matter.

**Signature spectacle:** Dark blood travels through cracks like roots, pale red fungal fans unfold across the soil, and an entire terrace sags quietly before collapsing into a steaming hollow. The boss’s slow scuttle makes the scale more disturbing than a fast leap.

**Prototype requirements:** Needs forecast wound thresholds, queued rupture events, three-stage terrain decay, a stable navigation circuit, reserved collapse destinations and a recoverable limit on concurrent erosion fronts.

## Rauk, the Last Bell

*Grey siege ogre*

**The bell that once warned a town of invasion now walks at the head of the invasion.**

**Tactical question:** Which squads take the bell swing, and who replaces them before the next one?

**Scale:** About 12 metres tall; proposed 3 × 3 collision footprint; 22 × 20 arena.

**Appearance:** A slab-bodied grey ogre beneath an asymmetrical timber-and-iron bell yoke. An enormous cracked bronze bell hangs beside its chest; one hand grips its rim and the other drags a gatehook. Scarred skin, blunt tusks, chain bites and faded red Host cloth. The bell is almost the size of a cottage.

**Place in the world:** Proposed Hillsvain siege beast. The Host has chained a stolen settlement alarm bell to Rauk and trained him to crush shield lines with it. Amberfeld scouts recognise the bell before they recognise the creature. His defeat lets that bell sound a warning again.

**Arena:** A ruined signal court on a high ridge, built around an old beacon foundation. Two broad approach lanes, freestanding counterweight stones and a wide turning apron make the fight readable. This is a new location, independent of the style-reference scenery.

**Movement:** Rauk advances at most two cells on an announced straight lane, then pivots in place. The swept footprint is shown. After each Bellbreaker he spends one beat recovering; after every second swing he changes which side of the court he threatens.

**Who takes which hit:** Two robust squads share Bellbreaker from separate marked front stations. A fresh guard takes the following targeted Gatehook. Spears in Square can hold a lane against the shove; otherwise a stone-backed brace position removes displacement. Crossbows and engines exploit the recovery beat.

### Attack set

| Attack | Warning | Resolution | Player response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gatehook | A chain points to a named squad in range; the target is locked for the next resolution. | One ordered physical missile attack. An adjacent Defend squad can intercept under the existing guard-selection rule. Armour matters; the forecast identifies the actual guard. | Choose a fresh guard or move the ward out of range. If the target becomes illegal, Rauk drags the empty hook back; he does not pick a new victim. |
| Bellbreaker | The raised bell and two linked shield stations show the exact two-tile impact front. | A fixed heavy impact budget is split between the two occupied stations. One participant receives the whole budget; extra squads outside the stations do not reduce it. Both recipients gain Ringing for two beats, increasing damage from a later Bellbreaker only. | Commit two healthy squads, then rotate them out. No Defend redirection of this shared AoE. An empty station converts its share into a visible shockwave lane behind it. |
| Toll of Ruin | Concentric numbered bands show an outer ring on this beat and an inner ring on the next. | The bell hits the ground. Each advertised ring deals damage and a one-cell push when its number resolves. | Move into the just-spent band, preserving facing for the next hit. Square or stone-backed footing prevents the push, not the damage. |
| Dragging the Clapper | A wide curved scrape footprint and the final facing are shown one beat ahead. | Rauk pivots and drags the hook across his old flank. The marked ground is struck; the other flank opens. | Rotate with the boss rather than camping permanently behind him. Keep a withdrawal gap between the two frontline squads. |
| The Last Toll | At the final phase, two beats of warning reveal a broad bell drop and the safe counterweight shadows. | The yoke breaks and the bell crashes down, leaving a low wreck. Rauk is staggered for the next full company turn. | Shelter behind the counterweights, then spend the opening on the now-unprotected chest. The wreck changes cover, not the navigable perimeter. |

### Phases

1. The Yoke: isolated Gatehook, Bellbreaker and Toll beats teach the three warning shapes.
2. Cracked Bronze, below roughly 65%: a scrape is announced for the beat after Bellbreaker, making the reserve route matter.
3. The Bell Falls, below roughly 30%: The Last Toll opens a final exposed phase. Rauk loses the shared bell strike but gains a clearly warned two-fist slam.

### Opening sequence

- Beat 1: Gatehook marks a rear squad; move a fresh Defend guard beside it.
- Beat 2: Bellbreaker stations appear. Move two robust squads into them and leave a central retreat gap.
- Beat 3: Recovery. Damaged recipients withdraw; ranged squads fire; reserve squads prepare the next face.
- Beat 4: Toll of Ruin begins outside, with the inner band already previewed. Move into the band that has just resolved.

**Typical failure:** A wounded squad stays in the next pair of Bellbreaker stations because it is already in position. Ringing turns an otherwise survivable assignment into multiple casualties.

**Recovery:** Ringing expires after two boss resolutions. The guaranteed recovery beat allows rotation or withdrawal; no requirement to resurrect or heal through lost soldiers.

**Signature spectacle:** The entire ogre leans into the bell swing; tiny shields meet its bronze rim, the yoke splits, and the familiar sound of a town alarm becomes a battlefield shockwave.

**Prototype requirements:** First mechanical prototype. Needs large footprints, locked intents, a two-station share, named guard preview, ring timing and deterministic phase scheduling.

## Veyr, the Cinder-Crowned

*Ancient dragon*

**Every breath destroys the shelter you were planning to use next.**

**Tactical question:** Where do you send the fire, and what will protect the company after that shelter is gone?

**Scale:** About 30 metres nose to tail; proposed 5 × 4 grounded footprint; 28 × 24 arena.

**Appearance:** An ancient heavy dragon with a low crocodilian head, a crown of broken backward horns, charcoal slate plates and an ember-lit breast visible between overlapping ribs of armour. Broad ochre wing membranes have old tears, thick forelimbs grip the ground, and a long weighted tail balances the creature. Four legs plus two wings, clearly separate.

**Place in the world:** Proposed old Nordmark dragon disturbed by the Host’s excavations. Cult survey stakes at the approach suggest someone wanted it awake, but Veyr is a sovereign animal rather than another obedient war machine. Its charred nest contains old frontier masonry, not a generic mountain of gold.

**Arena:** An open volcanic caldera containing ancient stone heat baffles and broad cool basalt shelves. The company fights at the rim of a natural furnace, not inside the generated cave reference. Three durable primary baffles and several weaker obstacles create a readable shelter economy.

**Movement:** Veyr turns through announced quarter-arcs, dragging the tail across the opposite side. After two breaths it bounds to a premarked basalt shelf, leaving the old breast position open. One warned wing crossing changes the firing axis without an off-screen damage phase.

**Who takes which hit:** A forward bait squad fixes the breath direction before evacuating. A fresh Defend guard intercepts Cinder Lance while the old guard sheds its heat. Other squads use real baffle shadows. Engines trade a turn of firing for reaching the next sustainable firing shelf.

### Attack set

| Attack | Warning | Resolution | Player response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Furnace Breath | The dragon watches a marked bait cell; its cone locks one full company turn before impact. | Fire strikes the locked cone and damages the first destructible cover in each ray. Protection is calculated from cover present at the start of that breath, so a baffle does not vanish and expose its shelter halfway through. | Bait the cone away from the next needed baffle, then leave it or reach a visible shadow. No invisible retargeting. |
| Cinder Lance | Veyr draws a spear-sized piece of vitrified slag into its jaws and marks a named squad in range. | It spits one heavy physical projectile at the marked unit. This ordered missile can be intercepted with Defend. The actual recipient becomes Heat-Soaked for two beats, increasing the next Cinder Lance impact only; the broad fire breath is unaffected by guard rules. | Assign a fresh adjacent guard, checking the actual recipient preview when several guards are eligible. Move a heated guard away from the ward. Cover may instead take this shot, at the cost of its displayed durability. |
| Crown and Tail | A frontal horn wedge and a later opposite tail crescent are numbered together. | The horn slam resolves first; the tail follows next beat, each with its own fixed ground shape. | Use the side pocket that survives both, rather than automatically fleeing behind the dragon. |
| Molten Footfalls | The bounding route, landing footprint and cooling duration are displayed. | The dragon lands and leaves a few molten prints. They become damaging only at the announced resolution, then cool after two beats. | Plan the engine move early and preserve the cool shelf as a rotation lane. The full wing silhouette is not a collision wall. |
| The Furnace Opens | The breast plates part; inward ash trails show an impending arena pulse two beats ahead. | The pulse strikes all unsheltered cells. The three primary baffles each survive their scheduled protection window; after the pulse Veyr’s breast stays exposed for a full company turn. | Split the company into the remaining shelter pockets, then focus damage. Shelter capacity and viable routes must exist for all surviving squads. |

### Phases

1. Banked Fire: a single breath teaches bait-and-lock and real cover shadows.
2. Cracked Furnace, below roughly 65%: breath direction and the next relocation are advertised together; Cinder Lance tests the reserve frontline.
3. The Crown Breaks, below roughly 30%: the breast stays vulnerable longer, but pulses accelerate. A late soft enrage consumes spare ground gradually rather than deleting all shelter at once.

### Opening sequence

- Beat 1: The bait squad chooses the first breath axis; everyone sees which baffle it would consume.
- Beat 2: The cone is locked. Evacuate the bait squad and settle ranged units on the next cool shelf.
- Beat 3: Fire resolves; put a fresh guard beside the Cinder Lance target without occupying tomorrow’s tail crescent.
- Beat 4: Breast recovery window. Fire the engines now that their preceding movement turn is over.

**Typical failure:** Every breath is survived, but each one is aimed through the only cover needed for the later arena pulse.

**Recovery:** Primary baffles have explicit durability and guaranteed first shelter windows. Minor cover can be spent to salvage a poor bait; the pulse forecast gives two full company turns.

**Signature spectacle:** The dragon’s chest opens like a furnace door; ash is pulled inward, banners point toward it, and six tiny formations disappear into the angular shadows of enormous basalt slabs.

**Prototype requirements:** Needs cover-shadow forecasting, cover durability snapshots per attack, grounded dragon footprints, temporal heat and safe-camera framing.

## The Widow of the King's Road

*Giant arachnid*

**The bridge is not wrapped in her web. The bridge is her web.**

**Tactical question:** Which paths will remain open after she pulls the battlefield tight?

**Scale:** About 20 metres across the legs; proposed 4 × 4 body footprint; 24 × 20 arena with eight mapped anchor points.

**Appearance:** An immense low black-brown spider with eight long articulated legs, a pale split saddle pattern on the abdomen and heavy ivory mouthparts. A hanging fan of coarse silk makes a royal-looking train. Old brass waymarks and snapped axle wood cling to the silk, never forming armour over every joint.

**Place in the world:** Proposed ancient predator of Nordmark trade routes. The Host has diverted traffic into her feeding grounds, turning a natural horror into a siege of the supply road. Her nest uses human construction as anchor points without implying human intelligence or a court of followers.

**Arena:** The exposed stone ribs of an unfinished aqueduct across a shallow chasm, joined by broad masonry terraces. Silk spans gaps above the fighting plane. Stable perimeter routes remain available; squads are not forced onto one-squad-wide bridges.

**Movement:** She walks between visible anchor clusters and can descend into an advertised landing footprint. A descent takes a full warning beat and never lands on unmarked units. Feet are spectacle outside the marked body footprint, not invisible collision walls.

**Who takes which hit:** Two squads hold separate tether posts when the web pulls; a mobile reserve cuts a route open. Armoured squads receive the bite, while crossbows attack the exposed underside during a lift. Every unit can cut a reachable silk knot by spending its act.

### Attack set

| Attack | Warning | Resolution | Player response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Widow Bite | A jaw marker locks the nearest reachable squad at announcement, with ties shown. | A heavy ordered melee attack and a short bleeding effect. Defend may intercept if the ward remains a legal target. | Use a robust guard and rotate before the next bite. Bleeding and the bite forecast include expected casualties. |
| Roadweaver | Two dashed silk lanes connect specific anchor points. | On the next resolution the lanes become sticky difficult ground. They cannot also erupt on that same beat. | Keep two routes open or cut one visible knot before the weave hardens. Do not park an engine in a corridor scheduled to close. |
| Draw the Loom | Two paired tether posts display separate load stations and arrows into the web. | Two stationed squads divide a fixed tension hit and keep the linked crossing intact. A missing station collapses that silk crossing; affected squads suffer the forecast pull to a safe masonry edge. | Assign sturdy squads to both posts or deliberately abandon that crossing and use the longer stone route. Square stops the pull but does not preserve the crossing or remove tension damage. |
| Pinned Shadows | Three numbered leg-tip shadows lock onto ground cells, rather than following units. | Each tip strikes its marked small cluster in the shown order. The last strike leaves her abdomen lifted for one company turn. | Move before the resolution, keeping a line of fire for the opening. One squad is never hit repeatedly merely for remaining in one overlapping visual leg. |
| The Hanging Road | Her descent point and the two web curtains are announced two beats in advance. | She hoists herself, drawing curtains across two routes before descending. The old body position opens as a safe crossing. | Rotate through the space she vacates; rescue a stranded rear squad by cutting a curtain. No spiderling wave is added. |

### Phases

1. The Toll Road: webs create manageable corridors while Bite establishes the frontline role.
2. The Loom, below roughly 65%: two anchor pairs become active, alternating the required holding positions.
3. Hanging Road, below roughly 30%: she lifts the centre of the web and changes position more often, but exposes her underside after every descent.

### Opening sequence

- Beat 1: Widow Bite and the first two prospective silk lanes are visible.
- Beat 2: Roadweaver hardens. Put a reserve beside the knot that would otherwise isolate the shooters.
- Beat 3: Draw the Loom resolves at the chosen pair of posts. Either hold both or take the longer masonry route.
- Beat 4: Pinned Shadows clears space and opens the underside; stop moving the engines before the firing window.

**Typical failure:** The whole company moves to a currently safe flank and discovers that the next web cuts its only exit.

**Recovery:** The outer masonry circuit remains connected. Losing a web bridge lengthens the route and costs an attack window; it is not an unannounced fall-to-death check.

**Signature spectacle:** A road-sized ribbon of silk lifts through the company, pulling empty wagons up like beads while the spider hangs over the arena on eight deliberate anchors.

**Prototype requirements:** Needs explicit temporary lane costs, destructible interactable knots, telegraphed landing footprints and a connected stable escape circuit.

## The Giant Troll

*Ancient woodland troll; new visual concept, encounter rules pending.*

**An ancient stone troll wakes beneath the ruined homes built on his sleeping shoulders.**

An ancient living-stone woodland troll with the supplied species' long drooping wedge-shaped nose, tiny amber eyes, heavy uneven brow, coarse dark hanging hair and immense shaggy beard. His head has no visible ears, stumps or earrings. Weathered grey mineral planes, shallow fissures, chipped knuckles, mottling and lichen replace fleshy skin while preserving chunky organic troll anatomy. Faded turquoise spirals remain. He stands with high hips, substantially straight legs, a broad chest and rounded hulking shoulders. The approved two roofless cream-plaster-and-stone cottage fragments, empty window holes and snapped timber remain across his back, with sparse old roots gripping their foundations. Their foundations merge into his mossy shoulders like remnants built on a sleeping rocky hill. No tall trees or intact houses.

**Scale:** Target: roughly seven metres, about four adult-human heights, with nearby soldiers on the same ground plane reaching roughly his knees.

**Arena:** An ancient northern woodland clearing with mossy stone, old oaks, distant pines and open earth paths.

**Reference:** The original central standing woodland troll is the direct species and rendering reference; the approved shoulder crop controls the ruins. Other Meridius characters were inspected for consistency. Intermediate takes 5 and 6 remain preserved with their visual limitations.
