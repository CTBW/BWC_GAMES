Visual direction, added gradually

The selected paintings govern the warm golden light, cool forest shade, soft atmospheric depth, natural road edges and mixed vegetation. Future passes should add more distinct tree species, ferns, shrubs, roots and mossy rocks, with a new in-engine comparison after each useful increment.

How to reach the painted look

The ground uses the existing continuous height surface. Five isolated paintings now supply the two woodland fields, while the other test fields keep the older foliage. The next art increment should improve ground texture scale and forest-edge composition, add a distinct birch and painted fallen timber, and reduce visible repetition of the tree silhouettes.

PartRecommended route
Trees, bushes, ferns, rocks and logsUse clean transparent painted cutouts, with consistent scale, root anchors, depth sorting and ground shadows. Start with the isolated concepts already saved.
Grass, dirt and roadsBlend painted surface textures and irregular road-edge masks across the terrain. Add smaller grass and verge cutouts for depth.
Hills and banksKeep real height data and a matching rendered surface so troops follow the ground. A pasted hill painting alone cannot establish elevation.
Golden forest atmosphereCombine varied canopy layers, warm highlights, cool shade, soft ground transitions and restrained mist. Judge the result at the gameplay camera.

Cutting objects from a full landscape can work when their complete shape is visible. Overlaps, baked lighting and fog make those extractions harder. We already have isolated oak, beech, pine, bush and fern concepts, so there is enough material to try this without new generation. Additional Higgsfield images should fill specific proven gaps. Tripo meshes are optional if the game later needs rotating views or full 3D.

Godot supports 2D image meshes and 2D lights and shadows. The route above is a recommendation for this game's fixed camera; exact visual fidelity remains to be demonstrated in the next art pass.

Layout inspiration

Open ground, forest pockets and visible hills draw on Age of Empires II. Clear routes, woodland boundaries and river crossings draw on Warcraft III and The Frozen Throne maps. Broad deployment slopes draw on Total War's terrain guidance. The ridge study also takes inspiration from the ridge and reverse-slope positions described by the National Army Museum's Waterloo account. These are design interpretations, not copied maps or imported combat mechanics.
