THE SANGUINE DELVER / A SPATIAL DAMAGE PUZZLE

Your damage changes the battlefield

Compare opening a wound in the central route with baiting the crawler onto expendable soil first. Advance the stages to see why the same successful attack can produce a very different position.

Erosion stage

  • S Stable stone outer route
  • B Boss footprint
  • 1–6 Company squads
  • × / ≋ / ▾ Stain / bloom / undercut
  • + Cauterised stable earth
  • F Quarry brazier

Illustrative schematic; not a playable map or a navigation simulation. Erosion stages each allow a full company turn. Squad markers show scale and formation, not a solved turn sequence.

Make the hard hit a decision

Read, assign, move

Warnings distinguish evacuate, intercept, share, bait and shelter. Every major move has a stated resolution turn. Boss movement, forced movement and the next escape route remain visible.

A shared hit uses separate squad stations. Defend still protects against eligible ordered attacks; it does not secretly absorb ground AoEs.

Losses carry weight

Soldiers who fall stay down. Major impacts can cause casualties even when handled well, but recovery beats and reserve rotations keep the fight from becoming an unavoidable drain on the roster.

One enemy and one health pool throughout. Body parts, roots, vents and conduits create tactical choices without spawning an army.

01 / Blood-crawler and fungal land-corruptor

The Sanguine Delver

Every wound you open becomes a wound in the land.

The decision

Where can you afford to wound it—and which ground must survive long enough to win?

A long, low rust-red crawler with a broad armoured head, paired hooked mouthparts and many short splayed legs. Overlapping dark-red shell plates rise into a few heavy fungal shelves; blood-filled translucent sacs hang beneath the flanks. Its pale underside contrasts with the red-black shell. It remains recognisably a scuttling animal beneath the fungus.

About 24 metres long; low, many-legged body; proposed 5 × 3 footprint; 26 × 24 arena.

Place in the world

Original boss inspired by the user’s small Drova creature reference, which shows a reddish, low, elongated crawler. Proposed Meridius ecology: Host tunnelling breaks into an old subterranean fungal colony; a centuries-old carrier reaches the surface, and its blood feeds a fungus that consumes the soil’s binding roots. The land slumps after the fungus hollows it. No claim about Drova’s actual lore or enemy mechanics is made.

The arena

A broad abandoned terraced quarry at the edge of exhausted farmland. Clay banks, shallow extraction channels and old stone haulways show progressive erosion clearly. An unbroken foundation-stone outer circuit is immune to the blood fungus; the valuable inner earth slowly becomes contested.

Movement

The crawler follows a forecast low S-shaped path toward a chosen blood-scent bait. Its whole swept body is marked. After two surface sequences it burrows shallowly, showing a moving earth ridge and an announced emergence footprint for a full turn. It cannot pass through stable foundation stone.

Who takes which hit

A sturdy frontal squad catches the maw while a separate pair holds pressure plates beside a fungal vent, dividing a rupture shock. A mobile squad carries the bait and preserves access to cauterising braziers. Shooters decide whether to cross a visible wound threshold now or wait until the crawler moves over expendable soil.

Phases and opening sequence

Escalation

  1. The Carrier: one stain front teaches the slow erosion sequence before any overlapping eruption.
  2. The Orchard of Blood, below roughly 65%: wound thresholds and vent assignments overlap. At most two active uncauterised erosion fronts advance at once; additional fronts wait visibly.
  3. The Hollowing, below roughly 30%: the shell opens and the crawler burrows more often. Underside attacks cancel queued ruptures, so aggression can reclaim control of the terrain.

First four beats

  1. Bait the crawler onto an outer earth terrace while an armoured squad prepares the stopping cell.
  2. Cross the first wound threshold there. The preview shows blood flowing away from the central haulway.
  3. The stain becomes Bloom. Two fresh squads hold the vent stations while a reserve reaches a brazier.
  4. Cauterise the most dangerous front; let the expendable terrace undercut. The next emergence exposes the underside and allows a queued rupture to be cancelled.
Failure, recovery and the signature moment

Failure: The company unloads every attack while the crawler straddles the central route, then discovers that its best damage turn has seeded the ground needed for the next two rotations.

Recovery: The stone outer circuit is permanently safe from erosion. Cauterising costs an act and access, not a rare class. Two-front scheduling bounds the puzzle; destroyed inner ground stays destroyed for the encounter, making earlier decisions matter.

The moment: Dark blood travels through cracks like roots, pale red fungal fans unfold across the soil, and an entire terrace sags quietly before collapsing into a steaming hollow. The boss’s slow scuttle makes the scale more disturbing than a fast leap.

Prototype needs: Needs forecast wound thresholds, queued rupture events, three-stage terrain decay, a stable navigation circuit, reserved collapse destinations and a recoverable limit on concurrent erosion fronts.

02 / Grey siege ogre

Rauk, the Last Bell

The bell that once warned a town of invasion now walks at the head of the invasion.

The decision

Which squads take the bell swing, and who replaces them before the next one?

A slab-bodied grey ogre beneath an asymmetrical timber-and-iron bell yoke. An enormous cracked bronze bell hangs beside its chest; one hand grips its rim and the other drags a gatehook. Scarred skin, blunt tusks, chain bites and faded red Host cloth. The bell is almost the size of a cottage.

About 12 metres tall; proposed 3 × 3 collision footprint; 22 × 20 arena.

Place in the world

Proposed Hillsvain siege beast. The Host has chained a stolen settlement alarm bell to Rauk and trained him to crush shield lines with it. Amberfeld scouts recognise the bell before they recognise the creature. His defeat lets that bell sound a warning again.

The arena

A ruined signal court on a high ridge, built around an old beacon foundation. Two broad approach lanes, freestanding counterweight stones and a wide turning apron make the fight readable. This is a new location, independent of the style-reference scenery.

Movement

Rauk advances at most two cells on an announced straight lane, then pivots in place. The swept footprint is shown. After each Bellbreaker he spends one beat recovering; after every second swing he changes which side of the court he threatens.

Who takes which hit

Two robust squads share Bellbreaker from separate marked front stations. A fresh guard takes the following targeted Gatehook. Spears in Square can hold a lane against the shove; otherwise a stone-backed brace position removes displacement. Crossbows and engines exploit the recovery beat.

Phases and opening sequence

Escalation

  1. The Yoke: isolated Gatehook, Bellbreaker and Toll beats teach the three warning shapes.
  2. Cracked Bronze, below roughly 65%: a scrape is announced for the beat after Bellbreaker, making the reserve route matter.
  3. The Bell Falls, below roughly 30%: The Last Toll opens a final exposed phase. Rauk loses the shared bell strike but gains a clearly warned two-fist slam.

First four beats

  1. Gatehook marks a rear squad; move a fresh Defend guard beside it.
  2. Bellbreaker stations appear. Move two robust squads into them and leave a central retreat gap.
  3. Recovery. Damaged recipients withdraw; ranged squads fire; reserve squads prepare the next face.
  4. Toll of Ruin begins outside, with the inner band already previewed. Move into the band that has just resolved.
Failure, recovery and the signature moment

Failure: A wounded squad stays in the next pair of Bellbreaker stations because it is already in position. Ringing turns an otherwise survivable assignment into multiple casualties.

Recovery: Ringing expires after two boss resolutions. The guaranteed recovery beat allows rotation or withdrawal; no requirement to resurrect or heal through lost soldiers.

The moment: The entire ogre leans into the bell swing; tiny shields meet its bronze rim, the yoke splits, and the familiar sound of a town alarm becomes a battlefield shockwave.

Prototype needs: First mechanical prototype. Needs large footprints, locked intents, a two-station share, named guard preview, ring timing and deterministic phase scheduling.

03 / Colossal corrupted dryad

Edratha, Mother of the Blight

She was the forest’s shelter. Now every refuge grows teeth.

The decision

Who carries the blight, where do they plant it, and which route must remain alive?

A recognisably feminine adult dryad formed from an enormous hollow living trunk, with a severe wooden face, long branch arms, and an asymmetrical antler-like crown. Black sap seams and muted wine-red leaves interrupt pale bark and old green moss. Several smaller dryad faces are grown into the trunk as a silent chorus, all part of one body.

About 18 metres tall; proposed 4 × 4 root-crown footprint; 24 × 22 arena.

Place in the world

Proposed matriarch of corrupted dryads. The Great Tyrants’ cult has driven triangular iron grafts into an ancient grove guardian. Her roots now preserve the grove by strangling everything that moves through it. The corruption is a wound with visible construction, rather than an unexplained evil palette swap.

The arena

A circular sanctuary of enormous interwoven roots around a sunken seed vault. Three pale uncorrupted root islands and broad bare-earth channels form the tactical ground. The environment is invented for this boss; the supplied terrain images do not define its setting.

Movement

Her central root crown stays planted during a sequence. Before the next sequence she uproots one side and pivots along a fully marked crescent. The old roots sink and open a route; new roots rise only after the warning turn.

Who takes which hit

A mobile, healthy squad carries the Blight Seed away from the formation. Two other squads receive the shared root crush. A reserve keeps a clean island accessible; ranged damage removes the exposed iron graft during recovery, counting toward the one boss health pool.

Phases and opening sequence

Escalation

  1. The Wounded Grove: one seed and one root pair teach separation and shared impacts.
  2. The Chorus, below roughly 65%: the embedded dryad faces wake; a seed bloom and corridor change overlap, with their order announced.
  3. Heartwood, below roughly 30%: the trunk splits around the iron graft. She reaches farther but grants a Pale Sap opening every other beat.

First four beats

  1. Blight Seed selects a mobile squad. Begin moving it toward a disposable edge pocket.
  2. Root Communion assigns two other squads. Keep the shortest return path to a clean island open.
  3. The seed plants; the Grove Closes preview shows why its placement matters.
  4. Pale Sap offers a cleanse and damage opening. Bring the carrier back without routing the company through its thorn patch.
Failure, recovery and the signature moment

Failure: The carrier drops a seed beside the only surviving passage because that cell is safe right now.

Recovery: A marked thin root can always be cut by an ordinary squad. The fight asks for actions and space, not a mandatory cleansing mage. Seed transfer preserves the original countdown.

The moment: The grove rises with her: roots arc over the soldiers like cathedral ribs, the faces in the trunk sing without mouths moving, and the iron corruption becomes visible inside a split living heart.

Prototype needs: Needs a transferable timed burden, temporary root obstacles, explicit growth displacement, cleanse stations and a damage window on the same boss.

04 / Ancient dragon

Veyr, the Cinder-Crowned

Every breath destroys the shelter you were planning to use next.

The decision

Where do you send the fire, and what will protect the company after that shelter is gone?

An ancient heavy dragon with a low crocodilian head, a crown of broken backward horns, charcoal slate plates and an ember-lit breast visible between overlapping ribs of armour. Broad ochre wing membranes have old tears, thick forelimbs grip the ground, and a long weighted tail balances the creature. Four legs plus two wings, clearly separate.

About 30 metres nose to tail; proposed 5 × 4 grounded footprint; 28 × 24 arena.

Place in the world

Proposed old Nordmark dragon disturbed by the Host’s excavations. Cult survey stakes at the approach suggest someone wanted it awake, but Veyr is a sovereign animal rather than another obedient war machine. Its charred nest contains old frontier masonry, not a generic mountain of gold.

The arena

An open volcanic caldera containing ancient stone heat baffles and broad cool basalt shelves. The company fights at the rim of a natural furnace, not inside the generated cave reference. Three durable primary baffles and several weaker obstacles create a readable shelter economy.

Movement

Veyr turns through announced quarter-arcs, dragging the tail across the opposite side. After two breaths it bounds to a premarked basalt shelf, leaving the old breast position open. One warned wing crossing changes the firing axis without an off-screen damage phase.

Who takes which hit

A forward bait squad fixes the breath direction before evacuating. A fresh Defend guard intercepts Cinder Lance while the old guard sheds its heat. Other squads use real baffle shadows. Engines trade a turn of firing for reaching the next sustainable firing shelf.

05 / Giant arachnid

The Widow of the King's Road

The bridge is not wrapped in her web. The bridge is her web.
