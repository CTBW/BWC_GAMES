MERIDIUS / TERRAIN KIT 2 / HIGGSFIELD NANO BANANA PRO / 24 OCTOBER 2026

Terrain kit 2: trees, fallen wood, stones, houses and ruins

Twenty new terrain subjects, painted to match the accepted terrain set: 10 trees in new species and colours, a sheet of 8 fallen-wood props (trunks, logs, stumps, a branch), a sheet of 10 small stones, 5 houses and 4 ruins. They were generated with the same prompt pack as the first terrain set. Each image was anchored on an asset that is already in the game: the oak for trees, the cottage for houses, the arch for ruins and the painted rock sheet for stones. The two Meridius battlefield paintings set the style for all of them. Every image stands on keyable magenta, so the game's own cutout tools can use it.

Status: the trees, stumps, logs, fallen wood and small stones are in the game (see In the game). The houses and ruins are not in yet. Nothing is committed; the PNG originals are kept locally.

Cost: 25 images for 58 credits (547.57 → 489.57). That covers the 4-image pilot, 19 new images and 2 fix edits. Model: Nano Banana Pro was requested on every job, but Higgsfield's job status reports nano_banana_2, as it did for the rock sheet already in the game.

In the game

The current battlefield is a summer woodland, so only summer trees joined it. Every new painting was keyed, graded to the battle's colours and cut into game sprites by tools/props/woodland_cutout.py (stones by tools/props/rock_cutout.py). The game picks a variant from each tile's random number, so the new variants appear in battles, on the campaign map and in the garrison. The rules and map layouts are unchanged.

Grading. The paintings were matched to the battle's palette in CIELAB: olive-khaki foliage in the light, teal in the shade, grey-brown bark. The birch's pale yellow crown is darkened and muted. The spruce and Scots pine lose their blue cast, more so in the light. The trunk sheet's orange bark is darkened and desaturated, and the fungus keeps its colour. The copper beech is a summer tree, but its dark red reads as autumn, so it sits with the autumn trees. It could join the summer woods as a rare accent.

At a glance

  • Strongest: the ruined chapel, the standing stones, the colonnade, the stone cottage, the windmill, the Scots pine, the copper beech, the red maple and the willow. Their brushwork is the same as the in-game oak's; in close-ups the maple's leaf clusters are almost indistinguishable from it.
  • Good with a small cutout fix: the barn has a detached blurred hay speck to its left, which the cutout should drop. The fixed fallen-wood sheet keeps a faint purple shadow beside the mossy log.
  • Fixed by an edit: the first wood sheet painted teal cast shadows onto the background, and the first copper beech stood on a purple ground patch. Neither could be keyed. One Nano Banana Pro edit of each (2 jobs, 6 credits) removed them and kept everything else.
  • Retaken: the pilot birch came back on dusty pink rather than magenta and was smoother than the oak. The retake has a clean background and matches the oak's brushwork. The second stone sheet replaces the pilot's gravestone-like waymarker and cartoon clover with natural stones.
  • Watch: the wood and stone props have slightly heavier outlines than the trees. Next to the grass the logs are warmer and more orange, and the new stones are paler than the in-game stones. Retexturing the small stones with the terrain's rock texture, as the in-game rocks were, would bring them in line.

At game scale

These are mock composites, not in-game captures. Each painting was keyed and given its cast shadow by the game's own cutout code (tools/props/woodland_cutout.py). It was then sized in world pixels and stood on the battle grass texture, beside sprites the game uses today (gold labels). A tile is 128 world pixels wide. Scroll sideways in a board, or click it to see it full size.

Trees

Ten new trees (3:4, 2K). The references were the accepted oak (image 1) and the two battlefield paintings. The prompts asked for the oak's brushwork, light, camera and framing, but not its species or silhouette.

Fallen wood

One 4K sheet with four large props on the top row (a fallen trunk about two tiles long, a hollow log with ferns, an old stump with bracket fungus, a stack of cut logs) and four small ones below (a mossy log with mushrooms, a sawn stump with an axe notch, a branch, a rotten stump). The references were the battlefield painting, the oak and the in-game rock sheet.

Small stones

Ten small stones, each a quarter to half a tile wide, to scatter between the larger rocks. The references were the battlefield painting and the in-game rock sheet.

Houses

The references were the accepted timber cottage and the two battlefield paintings. Each building stands on a small patch of its own grass, like the cottage.

Ruins

The references were the accepted ruined arch and the two battlefield paintings.

Where they could go in the game

The first proposal. The tree, stump, log and stone rows are now done as described in In the game. The ruins, houses and town rows are still open and wait for the next battlefields.
