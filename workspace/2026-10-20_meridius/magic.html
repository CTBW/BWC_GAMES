Meridius: magic and war machines

Status, 21 October 2026: mage squads for both sides and two war machines for each side are in the TypeScript game, with their spells, conditions, AI, effects, figures, HUD, recruitment and encounters. They play through the real interface without errors, 85 automated tests pass, and balance was checked by simulation. The user has not accepted the result yet. The same squads are now ported to the Godot version too (branch godot-migration, not committed; see Godot version).

What was added

  • Battle Mages (company, three to a squad): Chain Lightning, Frost Blast and Healing Light; promoted mages learn the Mending Circle. Weak arcane bolts between spells.
  • Goblin Shamans (host, three to a squad): Fireball, Bloodlust and Gutripper; hex bolts between spells.
  • War machines, each one engine and a crew of three (the crew is the squad's health and strength; the engine is wrecked when the last crewman falls):
    • Ballista (company): a great bolt that goes on through into the foe behind it.
    • Mangonel (company): a stone lobbed over walls and heads that smashes the squad struck and those beside it, friend or foe.
    • Rock Lobber (host): a cruder catapult that often lands astray.
    • Spiked War Wagon (host): rams a squad back out of the line and rolls into the gap.
  • One roll per squad. Every spell makes one hit roll; each squad it touches then takes one damage or healing roll, scaled by the casters standing (three mages cast three times as hard as one). Animations only play the result back.
  • Conditions: chilled (half movement, worse to hit, easier to hit), bloodlust (harder blows, no fear, lower guard), bleeding (loses HP and soldiers at the start of its next two turns).
  • Rules for machines: they cannot loose in a turn they have moved; lobbed stones need no line of sight but not closer than three tiles; a miss lands a tile astray and still strikes whoever stands there.
  • Where they appear: the Barracks offers one specialist squad each muster (mages, a ballista or a mangonel). Two new encounters, Shamans of the host and The host's war engines; a shaman marches with the warchief in the last stand.

Figures and engines

Godot version

Ported rule for rule to projects/meridius/source/godot on the godot-migration branch (worktree D:\ProjektM\BWC_GAMES-godot). 77 recorded battles, including both new encounters and a scripted company that casts every spell, replay identically to the TypeScript game: 30,361 events and 41,064 rule queries, random state included. Figures, engines, portraits, sounds and icons are baked from the TypeScript renderer. Captures below are from the Godot build at 1920×1080. The TypeScript version was archived on 21 October; Godot is the version developed from now on.

Checks: all 340 Godot tests pass, including 19 new rule tests for spells and machines; the exported Windows build passes its smoke test (all 13 encounters fought to an end, 42 sounds, 1,588 sprite variants); an input-driven play-through in a 1920×1080 window passed 236 checks. A headless stress run found a bug the original already had: two squads could end a move on one tile. The cause was a path crossing a routing enemy, or a free attack breaking a squad as it passed through an ally's tile. The rate was the same before and after the port (9 in 33 battles, 10 in 39). With the TypeScript version archived, it is fixed in Godot only: routing enemies block paths, and a squad cut short on an ally's tile falls back to the last tile it had to itself. The recorded test battles were re-recorded from Godot.

Deliberate differences from the TypeScript game: an engine stays drawn until its wreck appears (the original drops it a moment earlier); cancelling a spell with Esc clears its forecast at once; tall tooltips stay whole on screen. Full list: source/godot/MIGRATION.md, section “Magic and war machines”.

Balance

The standard army (two footmen, two spearmen) with a fifth squad of each kind, 120 battles per encounter (npm run sim -- specialists). All win 95–100%; the share of the army's HP left:

Battle mages68%68%79%60%74%
Ballista70%69%74%58%77%
Mangonel77%72%80%63%80%

Tuning on the way: mages were lost in 47% of battles at first (now about a quarter), ballistae idled after moving (now they hold their ground and loose about five and a half bolts a battle), and the mangonel won everything until its stone was cut to 10–14 with a smaller splash. Over whole campaigns (180 companies × 6 expeditions, two seed sets) 39.4% and 36.9% of expeditions succeed, against 40.8% and 41.0% before. A rock lobber and a shaman together in the last stand cost about six points, so the lobber was left out of it.

Checks run

  • npx tsc --noEmit clean; npx vitest run 85/85, 19 new in tests/spells.test.ts: one roll per squad, scaling with casters, chain arcs, chill timing, healing without resurrection, friendly fire and scatter, bloodlust, bleeding, the machines' setup rule, the bolt running through, lobbed stones, the wagon's smash, the AI casting on both sides, whole battles.
  • Battles without the new units play exactly as before (same win rates and losses in the simulator).
  • Every spell, shot and condition staged in the real renderer with no page errors; spells chosen, previewed and cast through the real interface (scripts/magicui.mjs).
  • Two full expeditions through the UI: no console errors, no failed requests.
  • The worst enemy turn with shamans and lobbers took under 10 ms headless.

Known limits

  • The art is drawn procedural figures like the rest of the game; engines have no wheel or arm animation beyond their loosing pose.
  • Glows baked into the staff and engine sprites cast faint shadows.
  • In a dense melee the condition labels beside the health bars can overlap their neighbours'.
  • The automated campaign player rarely hires specialists, so campaign balance with them in the company is untested.
  • Campaigns are about two to four points harder than before.
